      Install Ghaim on your phone

      iPhone: open this page in Safari, tap the Share button, then Add to Home Screen.

      Android: open it in Chrome, tap the menu, then Install app or Add to Home screen.

      After the first visit the sky model is stored on the phone, so cloud recognition also works without internet. The rain estimate then uses the photo only.

      How it works

      A neural network (EfficientNet-B0) running on the phone sorts the sky into one of seven types. It was trained on about 39,000 labelled sky photos, including about 20,000 phone photos from NASA's GLOBE Program. On everyday phone photos it had never seen, it names the sky type correctly 94% of the time; on the 8,975 test photos of the GCD research dataset, 78% of the time.

      The chance of rain combines three things: how often that sky type brings rain, how thick and dark the cloud looks (and, in a video, whether it grows or darkens), and the Open-Meteo forecast for your location: rain probability, expected rainfall, atmospheric instability and humidity. The estimate is a guide, not an official forecast.

      Privacy

      Photos are analysed on the phone and are never uploaded. Only your approximate location is sent to Open-Meteo to get the forecast. The log stays on this phone until you export it.
